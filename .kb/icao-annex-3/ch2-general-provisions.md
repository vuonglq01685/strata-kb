## 2 GENERAL PROVISIONS

Introductory Note 1.- The provisions of this Annex regarding meteorological information are subject to the understanding that a Contracting State's obligation, under Article 28 of the Convention on International Civil Aviation, is to supply meteorological information; responsibility for its use rests with the user.

Introductory Note 2.- Although the Convention allocates certain functions to the State of Registry, the Assembly, in Resolution A23-13, recognized that this State may be unable to adequately fulfil those responsibilities when aircraft are leased, chartered or interchanged (particularly without crew) by an operator of another State, and that the Convention may not adequately specify the rights and obligations of the State of the Operator until Article 83 bis of the Convention enters into force. The Council urged that in such instances, if the State of Registry cannot adequately discharge its Convention functions, it delegate to the State of the Operator, subject to that State's acceptance, those functions more adequately discharged by it. Pending entry into force of Article 83 bis, such delegation would be a matter of practical convenience only and would not affect the Convention's provisions on the duties of the State of Registry or any third State. Article 83 bis entered into force on 20 June 1997; transfer agreements have effect for Contracting States that have ratified the related Protocol (Doc 9318) upon fulfilment of the conditions established in Article 83 bis.

Introductory Note 3.- In the case of international operations effected jointly with aeroplanes not all registered in the same Contracting State, nothing in this Annex prevents the States concerned from entering into an agreement for the joint exercise of the functions placed upon the State of Registry by this Annex.

## 2.1 Objective, determination and provision of meteorological service

2.1.1 The objective of meteorological service for international air navigation shall be to contribute towards the safety, regularity and efficiency of international air navigation.

2.1.2 This objective shall be achieved by supplying operators, flight crew members, air traffic services units, search and rescue services units, airport managements and others concerned with international air navigation with the meteorological information necessary for their respective functions.

2.1.3 Each Contracting State shall determine the meteorological service it will provide to meet the needs of international air navigation, in accordance with this Annex and regional air navigation agreements; this includes determining service for international air navigation over international waters and other areas outside the State's territory.

2.1.4 Each Contracting State shall designate the authority, the meteorological authority, to provide or arrange for the provision of meteorological service for international air navigation on its behalf. Details of the designated meteorological authority shall be included in the State aeronautical information publication, in accordance with Annex 15, Chapter 5.

Note.- Detailed specifications on presentation and contents of the aeronautical information publication are given in the Procedures for Air Navigation Services - Aeronautical Information Management (PANS-AIM, Doc 10066), Appendix 2.

2.1.5 Each Contracting State shall ensure that the designated meteorological authority complies with World Meteorological Organization (WMO) requirements for qualifications, competencies, education and training of meteorological personnel providing service for international air navigation.

Note.- Requirements are given in the Technical Regulations (WMO-No. 49), Volume I, Part V (Qualifications and Competencies of Personnel) and Part VI (Education and Training of Meteorological Personnel), and Appendix A (Basic Instruction Packages).

## 2.2 Supply, use, quality management and interpretation of meteorological information

2.2.1 Close liaison shall be maintained between those supplying and those using meteorological information on matters affecting the provision of meteorological service for international air navigation.

2.2.2 Each Contracting State shall ensure that the designated meteorological authority referred to in 2.1.4 establishes and implements a properly organized quality system comprising procedures, processes and resources for the quality management of meteorological information supplied to the users listed in 2.1.2.

2.2.3 Recommendation.- The quality system established under 2.2.2 should conform to the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 9000 series of quality assurance standards and should be certified by an approved organization.

Note.- The ISO 9000 series provides a basic framework for a quality assurance programme; details are formulated by each State and are usually unique to it. Guidance is given in the Guide to the Implementation of Quality Management Systems for National Meteorological and Hydrological Services and Other Relevant Service Providers (WMO-No. 1100).

2.2.4 Recommendation.- The quality system should assure users that meteorological information complies with stated requirements for geographical and spatial coverage, format and content, time and frequency of issuance, period of validity, and accuracy of measurements, observations and forecasts. When the quality system indicates non-compliance and automatic error correction is not appropriate, such information should not be supplied unless validated with the originator.

Note.- Requirements are given in Chapters 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 and Appendices 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9 of this Annex and relevant regional air navigation plans; accuracy guidance is in Attachments A and B.

2.2.5 Recommendation.- For exchange of operational meteorological information, the quality system should include verification and validation procedures for monitoring adherence to prescribed transmission schedules for messages/bulletins and their filing times, and should be capable of detecting excessive transit times of messages and bulletins received.

Note.- Requirements are given in Chapter 11 and Appendix 10 of this Annex.

2.2.6 Compliance of the quality system shall be demonstrated by audit. If non-conformity is identified, action shall be initiated to determine and correct the cause. All audit observations shall be evidenced and properly documented.

2.2.7 Owing to variability of meteorological elements in space and time and limitations of observing techniques and element definitions, the specific value of any element in a report shall be understood by the recipient as the best approximation of actual conditions at the time of observation.

Note.- Guidance on operationally desirable accuracy of observation is given in Attachment A.

2.2.8 Owing to variability of meteorological elements and limitations of forecasting techniques and element definitions, the specific value of any element in a forecast shall be understood as the most probable value likely during the forecast period; a time given in a forecast shall be understood as the most probable time.

Note.- Guidance on operationally desirable accuracy of forecasts is given in Attachment B.

2.2.9 Meteorological information supplied to the users listed in 2.1.2 shall be consistent with Human Factors principles and in forms requiring a minimum of interpretation by these users.

Note.- Guidance on application of Human Factors principles is in the Human Factors Training Manual (Doc 9683).

## 2.3 Notifications required from operators

2.3.1 An operator requiring meteorological service or changes to existing meteorological service shall notify, sufficiently in advance, the meteorological authority or aerodrome meteorological office concerned. The minimum advance notice required shall be as agreed between the meteorological authority or aerodrome meteorological office and the operator concerned.

2.3.2 The meteorological authority shall be notified by the operator requiring service when new routes or new types of operations are planned; lasting changes are made in scheduled operations; and other changes affecting the provision of meteorological service are planned. Such information shall contain all details necessary for the authority's planning of appropriate arrangements.

2.3.3 The operator or a flight crew member shall ensure that, where required by the meteorological authority in consultation with users, the aerodrome meteorological office concerned is notified of flight schedules; when non-scheduled flights are to be operated; and when flights are delayed, advanced or cancelled.

2.3.4 Recommendation.- The notification to the aerodrome meteorological office of individual flights should contain the following, except that for scheduled flights the requirement for some or all of it may be waived as agreed between the office and the operator: aerodrome of departure and estimated time of departure; destination and estimated time of arrival; route to be flown and estimated arrival/departure times at intermediate aerodrome(s); alternate aerodromes needed to complete the operational flight plan, taken from the relevant list in the regional air navigation plan; cruising level; type of flight (visual or instrument flight rules); type of meteorological information requested by a flight crew member (flight documentation and/or briefing or consultation); and time(s) at which briefing, consultation and/or flight documentation are required.
