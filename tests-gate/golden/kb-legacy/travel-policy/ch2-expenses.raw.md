## 2.1 Expense classes

Travel expenses fall into three classes. Class A covers transport booked through the preferred vendor. Class B covers lodging within the city cap published each January. Class C covers meals and incidentals, receipted, up to the daily cap. Anything outside these classes is a Class X item and needs director approval before the trip, not after.

City caps are published on the intranet by the first working day of January and apply to trips starting that calendar year. A booking that exceeds the Class B cap by less than 10 percent may be accepted when no in-cap room is available within 2 km of the workplace; the booking notes must attach the three declined quotes. Alcohol is never Class C. Client entertainment is a separate policy and must not be coded as travel. Mileage for a private car, when a preferred-vendor option exists, is Class X.

## 2.2 Booking window

Domestic trips must be booked at least 7 calendar days in advance. International trips must be booked at least 21 calendar days in advance. A trip booked inside the window is still allowed when the manager records a reason in the booking tool. Last-minute changes after tickets are issued are charged to the requesting team, not to the central travel budget.

The clock starts on the day the booking is submitted, not the day it is approved. Weekend days count. A change of dates that keeps the same tickets is not a new booking; a change that issues new tickets is. International includes any destination outside the country of the employee's home office. Rail that crosses a border is international. The travel desk may refuse an inside-window booking that lacks a manager reason; the employee then rebooks or cancels.
